# The tree-sitter ecosystem for Exercism's tracks

This file lists, for each of Exercism's 85 active tracks, which tree-sitter grammar exists, who owns it, whether it is actively maintained, which ABI it targets and whether nvim-treesitter ships it.

All data was pulled on 2026-11-26 through the GitHub API (read only). "Last commit" is the last commit on the default branch. It is not the repo's `pushed_at` date, because many tree-sitter org repos show a push in September 2026 with no new commits on their main branch.

## Counts

| Rating | Tracks |
|---|---|
| Active | 42 |
| Slow but alive | 37 |
| Stale | 3 |
| None | 3 |

The ratings use each track's recommended grammar. The rule for each rating is:

- Active means a commit in the last 6 months and maintainers who respond.
- Slow but alive means a commit 6 to 24 months ago, or recent commits with no maintainer replies to the issues and PRs that came in.
- Stale means nothing for 24 months, or an archived repo.
- None means no grammar was found.

A repo with recent commits and no incoming issues or PRs counts as responsive, because nobody was waiting for a reply. "Maintainers responded" means a comment from an owner, member or collaborator, a merged PR, or a closed issue in the last 6 months, among each repo's 8 most recently updated issues and 8 most recently updated PRs. This test is rough. Ratings near a boundary deserve a second look before anyone depends on them.

## Parse rates

The Parse rate column shows the share of a track's own example and exemplar solutions (every non-test file with the track's extension under `.meta/`, or `examples/` for PureScript) that parse with no `ERROR` or `MISSING` node. The count of files is in brackets. The tests used tree-sitter CLI 0.25.10. The grammars pinned by Inspector were tested at their pinned commit, and the others at their latest commit on 2026-11-26. The rate is the highest for a grammar in the table, and measures parsing of well-formed example code only. It does not measure the quality of node names or the stability of the tree shape, and student code will parse worse. The column says "not measured" for the 39 tracks that were not tested and the 3 tracks with no grammar. The Notes column names the first construct that broke each failing file, so a file may contain more failing constructs.

## Summary of the data

These counts describe 82 of the grammars in the table. They leave out the grammars recommended for arturo, awk, ballerina, cairo, factor, jq, pharo-smalltalk and wasm.

- 64 of the 82 grammars ship `queries/highlights.scm`. Another 8 ship a `queries/` folder without that file, and 10 have no queries folder.
- 56 of the 82 are in nvim-treesitter's main branch. The master branch is frozen at 2026-03-23, and main was last updated on 2026-11-21. In main every parser has a tier (stable, unstable, unmaintained). Only three of our grammars are marked unmaintained: gdscript, roc and unison. Only csharp and python are marked stable.
- 32 grammars have a committed `parser.c` at ABI 14 or lower. Four of them are at ABI 13: factor, jq (the nvim-treesitter one), pharo-smalltalk and wasm. libtree-sitter 0.25 loads ABI 13 to 15. Inspector's own requirement is ABI 15 (libtree-sitter 0.25), so ABI 14 grammars load, and the ones that need regenerating are easy to spot in the ABI column.
- 9 grammars do not commit `parser.c` and need `tree-sitter generate` at build time, as Inspector already does for raku: abap, arturo, cfml, perl5, prolog, raku, sqlite, swift and vbnet. For these the ABI column shows the CLI version named in `package.json`, which is what decides the ABI.

## Language name mappings

- csharp is `c_sharp` in nvim-treesitter and `c-sharp` in the repo name.
- common-lisp is `commonlisp` (Inspector calls it `common_lisp`).
- emacs-lisp is `elisp`. nvim-treesitter does not have it.
- perl5 is `perl`. raku is a separate grammar, and nvim-treesitter does not have it.
- vimscript is `vim`, in the `tree-sitter-grammars/tree-sitter-vim` repo.
- objective-c is `objc`.
- free-pascal and delphi both use `pascal`.
- wasm is `wat` (Inspector's name too).
- vlang is `v`, and it lives inside the `vlang/v-analyzer` repo.
- sqlite uses `sql`, a general SQL grammar.
- arm64-assembly and x86-64-assembly both map to the generic `asm` grammar, and x86-64-assembly may prefer `nasm`.
- reasonml has no `reason` entry in nvim-treesitter. nvim-treesitter's `ocaml` is for OCaml syntax only.
- pharo-smalltalk maps to the `smalltalk` grammar by tom95, which nvim-treesitter does not ship.
- factor has nothing in nvim-treesitter. Its `forth` parser is a different language.

## Risks

Tracks where the choice of grammar was unclear:

- arm64-assembly and x86-64-assembly (generic assembly, ARM64-specific or NASM-specific grammars all exist)
- dart (two live repos, the established one and a much busier newer one)
- delphi and free-pascal (the Pascal grammar covers both, and a Delphi-only grammar exists but is new)
- moonscript (two near-identical 1-star repos, and neither parses any solution)

Grammars that depend on one maintainer, where over 70% of the last 100 commits in 12 months came from one human: abap, lean, unison, red, scheme, racket, idris, powershell, kotlin, roc, wren, erlang, php, ocaml, r, gdscript, rust and the tree-sitter-grammars repos that clason maintains (c, lua, vim). Several official tree-sitter org repos (c, cpp, ruby, rust, haskell, php) also have only 1 to 4 committers in the last 12 months, and the same names (maxbrunsfeld, amaanq, clason) appear across several of them. If a few people leave, many grammars could go quiet together.

Forks and moves:

- nvim-treesitter main now points c, julia and haskell at `tree-sitter-grammars` repos. The c fork is 2 commits ahead of `tree-sitter/tree-sitter-c`. The julia and haskell forks have diverged from the official repos (14 ahead and 10 behind for julia, 9 ahead and 12 behind for haskell). Inspector pins the official `tree-sitter` repos for the languages it already has, so the choice of repo matters for these three.
- tree-sitter-grammars/tree-sitter-cairo is a fork of a repo that doesn't exist any more. The recommended grammar is the StarkWare one.
- Zig: Inspector pins Exercism's fork `exercism/tree-sitter-zig`. `maxxnino/tree-sitter-zig` is archived (last commit 2024-10-13, ABI 14), and nvim-treesitter uses `tree-sitter-grammars/tree-sitter-zig` (ABI 15, last commit 2025-09-10).
- The official `tree-sitter/tree-sitter-swift` is archived. nvim-treesitter and everyone else use `alex-pinkus/tree-sitter-swift`.
- The clojure repo has moved to `clj-commons/tree-sitter-clojure`, and `sogaiu/` still redirects to it.

Grammars already pinned by Inspector that are stale or at risk:

- wasm: the pin is Exercism's fork of `g-plane/tree-sitter-wat` (ABI 15), which adds wabt's folded `if` shorthand. The alternative, wasm-lsp/tree-sitter-wasm, had its last commit on 2022-05-17, targets ABI 13 and has 8 open PRs.
- zig: the pin is Exercism's fork (see above). The archived maxxnino grammar and the nvim-treesitter one both parse all 104 solutions.
- wren: 1 star, one author, last commit 2026-02-28. The pinned commit is the latest, and it parses all 313 real solutions in the golden data.
- raku: 0 stars, 2 commits, created in the last month. It is still the only Raku grammar that parses real code (69.2%).
- gdscript: marked unmaintained in nvim-treesitter. The repo still gets commits, mostly from a contributor who is not the owner.
- crystal: no commits since 2025-10-12 and 6 open issues without replies.
- go, java, javascript, python, ruby, typescript, rust, common-lisp and nim have no commit in the last 6 months. These are mature official grammars, and the pins make updates deliberate, so this is low risk. Ruby (ABI 14), Java (14) and TypeScript (14) have not been regenerated for ABI 15 yet. They parse 99% to 100% of their track's solutions, but each lacks some syntax from the last two releases of its language (see the Notes column).

Other risks:

- Three grammars have many recent commits authored by an account named "claude" (cfml, perl5 and emacs-lisp). Their activity numbers are high, and the grammar may change a lot between pins.
- Several grammars are generic and do not match the track: sqlite (general SQL), the two assembly tracks, delphi (Pascal), pharo-smalltalk (generic Smalltalk). A tree-sitter grammar for these tracks will parse more than the track teaches, or fail on the track's dialect.
- Few grammars belong to the language's own organisation. Of those 82 repos, 42 belong to individuals, 24 to the tree-sitter or tree-sitter-grammars orgs, 9 to community orgs, 5 to a language org (elixir-lang, gleam-lang, rescript-lang, red, vlang) and 2 to a company.
- 18 of those 82 grammars have no `queries/highlights.scm` (8 have only other query files, 10 have no queries folder). Inspector's tree-sitter prototype relies on each grammar's own `highlights.scm` and `locals.scm`, so these tracks need their queries written.
- Release tags are rare. 20 of those grammars have no tag or release at all (including arturo, crystal, dart, lean, raku, red, roc, tcl and wren), so pinning has to be to a commit.
